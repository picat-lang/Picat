\section*{Acknowledgements}
The initial design of Picat was published in December 2012, and the first alpha version was released in May 2013. Many people have contributed to the project by reviewing its ideas, design, implementation, or documentation. We thank Roman Bart'{a}k, Nikhil Barthwal, Mike Bionchik, Lei Chen, Veronica Dahl, Claudio Cesar de S'{a}, Agostino Dovier, Sergii Dymchenko, Julio Di Egidio, Christian Theil Have, H{\aa}kan Kjellerstrand, Annie Liu, Nuno Lopes, Marcio Minicz, Richard O'Keefe, Lorenz Schiffmann, Paul Tarau, and Jan Wielemaker.

We are especially grateful to H{\aa}kan Kjellerstrand, who has been programming in Picat and blogging about it since May 2013. The hundreds of Picat programs he has shared at \url{https://github.com/hakank/hakank/} helped the system mature quickly.

We also thank Bo Yuan (Bobby) Zhou for designing the Picat website (\url{https://picat-lang.org}), Sanders Hernandez for implementing the interface to the FANN neural network library, and Domingo Alvarez Duarte for porting Picat to MinGW (\url{https://github.com/mingodad/picat}).

Finally, we thank Cristian Grozea for creating Picat's experimental branch (\url{https://github.com/picat-lang/Picat/tree/experimental}), which hosts several early-stage projects. Some of his experiments led to features now available in the official branch, including lambda definitions and the external SAT interface.

The Picat implementation is based on the B-Prolog engine. It uses the following public domain modules: \ignore{\texttt{prism} by Taisuke Sato and Yoshitaka Kameya; }\texttt{token.c} by Richard O'Keefe; \texttt{getline.c} by Chris Thewalt; \texttt{Espresso} (by Berkeley); \texttt{FANN} by Steffen Nissen. In addition, Picat also provides interfaces to the SAT solver Kissat (\url{https://github.com/arminbiere/kissat}), \texttt{Gurobi} by Gurobi Optimization, Inc, \texttt{Cbc} by John Forrest, \texttt{GLPK} by Andrew Makhorin, \texttt{SCIP} by the Zuse Institute, and \texttt{z3} by Microsoft.

The Picat project was supported in part by the NSF, CUNY RF, and others. % under grant numbers CCF1018006 and CCF1618046.
